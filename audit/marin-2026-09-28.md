## Communication experiment: score depends on what is measured

The [new #8317 follow-up](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/8317#issuecomment-5860234620) qualifies [open PR #9481](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/9481). Rank 0 reports 81.0% communication overlap; rank 37 reports 72.5%. Their compute spans are 12.18 and 11.55 seconds respectively. These traces were taken in separate runs and may have landed on different racks, so they do not provide a simultaneous multi-rank comparison.

The faster rank spends more time waiting for peers in collectives. Removing one synchronizing collective can move the wait rather than remove it. A further variant reduced overlap to 70.5% and increased step time 13.72→14.0 seconds; its profile-guided schedule developed about 1e-3 loss drift by step 144021, whereas the same program without that schedule matched baseline within 1e-4. The author suspects a race, not a confirmed cause, and says these additional changes will not land. Other rejected scheduling variants are documented in the public comment.

The original PR remains open with its earlier one-rack 13.72 versus 13.94-second result. The assertion that 81% is a practical scheduling limit is the author's current belief, not a mathematical bound or whole-rack metric. Tracing several ranks in one run is the proposed next measurement. No eleven-rack performance or deployment claim follows.

## Evaluation: preserve infrastructure failure identity

[Open PR #9484](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/9484) reports a September 26 math500 run with 288 model-transport failures among 500 source samples. Rebuilding the archive replaced failure markers with empty outputs and reported 500/500 scored with no errors. The proposal preserves failure categories, recomputes metrics over successfully scored items, and marks affected runs `infra_failed` when scored coverage is below 90% of intended attempted items. Metrics and coverage remain retained; failed runs should not be cached as successful. This is a reported evaluation-system defect, not evidence that Hero's live Paloma or UncheatableEval values are erroneous.

It reports processing 292 sources, 18,710,478,349 documents and 162,535 shards in 2h50m, plus an exact row-order/score/type/bucket check on `nsf_awards` (381789 documents). A scoring rerun against the same embeddings differed by at most 1.6e-3 (mean 2.3e-4), attributed by the author to bf16 reduction order with different batch sizes. Historical August tokenized inputs were deleted; their existing score outputs are registered by path map rather than regenerated from those deleted inputs. Separate scorer and calibration fingerprints support provenance, but this does not prove a changed main-run mixture or reproducibility of deleted original inputs.

These remain public agent-reported experiments; they were not independently reproduced.
