## Findings, measurements and limits

### Hero has switched to a four-times-longer reading window

**confirmed** — Hero’s main run now trains with a 65K-token reading window, four times its previous 16K window. Official launch and initial operating gates are supported by the new public run, but successful startup does not yet prove long-context capability or recovery from the child’s own checkpoint. This advances yesterday’s proposal to confirmed production adoption. PR #10820 merged at November 2 02:55:41 UTC as 6e6924fedd64cc241f2de50ec3660a386ec12436. The parent was intentionally cancelled after its control window through 372049; the 65K child hero-ctx65k-step372k launched at 03:25 UTC (November 1, 22:25 New York), restored permanent checkpoint 372000, and reached 372102 on attempt zero. The launch identifies eleven NVL72 racks, CP1 × EP64, receiver capacity 1.5, attention scaling qk_mult 1.66 and W&B fork through parent step 371999. Initial gates passed: first-ten-step maximum cross entropy 1.007 < 1.25; child-minus-parent control loss −0.02009 within 0.05; warmed median step 16.05 seconds < 17.3; final gate-window mean dropped-assignment fraction 0.0004058 < 0.002; peak memory 127.8 GiB < 130; no sustained gradient spike or retry reported. The metric table uses different windows and statistics: warmed-window mean step 16.11 seconds, not the median 16.05. Higher MFU is not a speedup: longer attention costs more compute, while tokens per second fell relative to 16K. Lower control loss also uses a different context and is not an isolated quality gain. At November 2 13:57:43.884 UTC (08:57:43 EST), anonymous W&B independently shows the child running at 374269 with context 65536, capacity 1.5, qk_mult 1.66 and gradient cap 1.0: loss 0.9914590, training 16.0331 seconds, iteration 16.1487 seconds, MFU 33.4219%, median 33.3242% over 500 samples. Its latest returned Paloma/UncheatableEval values are 0.7182593/0.4712321 BPB at 371999, inherited from the 16K parent; they are not new 65K evaluations. The first child evaluation at 375000 and permanent save at 378000 remain pending in the inspected official status. November 5 completion is a conditional forecast, not measured completion. The parent’s W&B state says crashed, while official status records deliberate cancellation; that label alone does not establish a new training incident.

Concept: A larger reading window changes both training cost and the information available to the model. Startup gates, long-context quality and checkpoint recovery need separate evidence.

### Proposed data checks would stop silently missing important evaluation sets

**planned** — Two open data-cleaning changes would make contamination checks harder to bypass accidentally, including when an evaluation item is hidden inside a long document. Neither changes Hero’s already pinned data-cleaning paths, so they do not certify its training data clean or repair its historical scores. PR #10541 makes required evaluation-suite loading fail closed before sealing a corpus or building its membership filter. Its public account says earlier CoreWeave marks never checked MMLU or GSM8K because best-effort loading skipped failures, and UncheatableEval was absent. This is a coverage gap, not a measured contamination rate or proof a specific score is inflated. The author reports v5 staged and sealed in four regions with nine AA benchmarks, seven Uncheatable subsets and 811 lm-eval leaf tasks; 116 other best-effort leaves still fail. A later reply supersedes the earlier missing-stage review and fixes viewer family handling without renaming sealed IDs; private regional manifests were not independently accessed. Stacked PR #10836 adds an item-level 13-gram overlap rule so surrounding text cannot dilute a copied evaluation item. Proposed thresholds are 0.5 for MMLU/MMLU-Pro/Uncheatable, 0.4 for HumanEval and 0.3 for GSM8K; boilerplate hashes do not contribute toward the threshold. The v6 corpus is not staged, and review identifies report/viewer attribution and a missing-configuration guard as remaining behavior gaps. Local 527 passing / five expected-failure tests are reported, not a production canary or Hero mixture adoption. No independent reproduction of broad recall, precision or giant-document memory claims is asserted.

Concept: A contamination check can miss a benchmark because the benchmark never loaded, or because the matching rule misses its form. Neither absence of a flag proves the training data are clean.

Exercise: What should a data-cleaning pipeline do when a required evaluation dataset cannot be loaded?

### An open repair would preserve some evaluation work before a timeout

**planned** — A proposed repair would periodically save completed evaluation results instead of keeping everything in memory until the last task finishes. It could reduce lost work after interruption, but backups are best effort and the open PR does not establish durable recovery of every result. Issue #10837 observed 1522 of 1626 Mini840 rollouts completed after 71580 seconds against an 86400-second job limit, while the final export path was empty. That shows a loss risk, not proof this run actually timed out and lost every result. Its 401 retained agent trials are failure-selected, not a representative substitute for the completed set. After feedback, #10840 replaced an earlier immutable-per-row, fail-on-upload proposal with local append-only JSONL and asynchronous backups to two fixed keys per attempt, with a 30-second upload deadline. Storage errors do not stop the evaluation, and a kill can lose work since the last successful upload. Retry keys now preserve older attempts. Step-wise rows retain is_last_step in partial and full dumps; score readers must select final-step rows rather than average all rows. Local prefix/retry/deadline tests, CI and wheel packaging are reported passing. The vendored runtime wheel needs the change, but the PR remains open awaiting review, with no deployed recovery result or complete Mini840 quality score. Nearby #10737 serializer changes still need coordination.

Concept: A partial backup preserves a known prefix, not a full benchmark. Counts, attempt identity and selection rules determine which results can honestly be scored.

Exercise: If only 1500 of 1626 tasks survive, what must accompany any score reported from those tasks?
