### A Snowball test makes replies repeatable, with a substantial speed cost

**experimental** — A separate Snowball serving experiment made the same 164 code-test replies repeat across repeated launches and request orders. The result depends on several numerical controls and takes about three times longer, so it is not a deployed Hero improvement or a portable guarantee for other models. The public report uses Snowball v0.1 on four H100s with data and expert parallelism at vLLM e09cfd55 plus local controls: batch-invariant router arithmetic, fixed-root expert-output reduction, deterministic compilation, and no one-token CUDA graph. It reports 13 passes over four launches, 16 and 64 requests in flight, and four orders, all matching 164 HumanEval+ replies. A pass at 64 in flight took about 300 seconds versus 100 with default serving and 1,000 in eager mode. Router control raised eager matching from 23/164 to 161/164; compilation left two placement-sensitive replies until the size-one graph was removed. Sliding-window attention on long, chunked prompts adds a separate alignment limit; long-prompt scoring disabled chunked prefill. The reporter explicitly did not run revision 01911be3, current #10760 revision 39e628, or its DP8/EP8 topology. Separately, #9558 traces historical requests to effective temperature 1.0 without a seed because the pinned client omitted both fields. Greedy decoding alone did not make serving repeatable. Noise estimates and other-model controls cannot conclusively explain all historical score differences. Draft #10760 remains open; deployment and general repeatability remain unverified.

Concept: The same prompt and greedy decoding do not guarantee the same arithmetic under changing batches. Repeatability has a configuration and a performance cost.

Exercise: If a deterministic serving configuration is three times slower, what must be held fixed when comparing its scores with the earlier server?
