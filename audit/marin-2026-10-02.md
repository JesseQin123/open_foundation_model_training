## Checkpoint cost: small test does not reproduce the full slowdown

[October 1 checkpoint study](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/9180#issuecomment-5940869951), newly reviewed today, analyzes 79 hourly saves at steps 176000–188824. It reports median excess 19 seconds across the next eleven steps, or 22 seconds across thirty, with a 15-second baseline and roughly 90-second slowdown tail. About 2.4 seconds is blocking staging; the estimated hourly cost is approximately 0.55% of training time.

One rack of 64 GB200 GPUs writes the production per-rank share, about 8 GiB, using an emulated eight-replica planner (0.552 TB/save). Settings cycle on one allocation every four minutes. Excess time across the save and next eight steps: usual 8.9 s (2 saves), deferred purge 8.5 s (3), request concurrency 128 at 8.3 s (2), copy concurrency 4 at 10.2 s (2), no-save −0.2 s (2). About three seconds in saving arms is an extra directory-listing budget-check artifact. Purge calls drop from about 141k to 2.4k–3.9k, but the time improvement is not meaningful with these tiny samples.

The production explanation—roughly 580 writers serially committing shared metadata and freeing buffers at different times, with collective waits amplifying stalls—is the author's hypothesis. The one-rack tail is short and cannot test that mechanism. The harness branch is unpublished; its code and internal logs were not independently reviewed. The public comment is evidence of the reported experiment, not verification of every underlying trace.

The approximately 40-minute Young/Daly interval is calculated using an earlier 7.3-day/four-failure window and ~20-second save cost: modeled save/rework cost 1.69% at 60 minutes, 1.59% at 40, 1.68% at 30, 2.5% at 15. It is neither a production schedule change nor an updated estimate incorporating today's retries.
