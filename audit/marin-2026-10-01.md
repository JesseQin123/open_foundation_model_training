## Longer inputs: small throughput cost, limited evidence

[Experiment #9615](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/9615) restores permanent checkpoint 180000 on one rack, 16 nodes × four GPUs, EP64, ragged all-to-all, capacity factor 1.15. Source revision is `f38da1173de79f1eacd5f54bcfb88db6cb0a9f6e`. Each arm runs three seeds × 100 steps, with reported statistics from the final 90 steps. Tokens per step are fixed at 4194304; sequence batch sizes are 1024/512/256 for 4K/8K/16K. No evaluation or checkpoint write occurs in the tests.

| Context | Tokens/s | Relative throughput | MFU | Dropped expert assignments |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4K | 301.9k ± 0.2k | reference | 28.25 ± 0.02% | 1.86e-4 ± 0.08e-4 |
| 8K | 297.0k ± 0.2k | −1.6% | 28.47 ± 0.02% | 8.29e-4 ± 0.39e-4 |
| 16K | 292.5k ± 0.3k | −3.1% | 29.37 ± 0.03% | 2.94e-3 ± 0.20e-3 |

All drops are sender-side; receiver drops are zero. These are assignment drops, not removal of entire data tokens. Measured peak memory is 103.1 GiB/GPU in every arm despite larger compiler memory estimates. Utilization rises because the analytic FLOP count charges more attention work at longer lengths; throughput falls. A higher MFU is therefore not evidence of faster data processing.

Boundaries: attention scale changes with length (1.3/1.39/1.48); the diagnostic peak learning rate is 0.69 times Hero's, and attention-gate/router weight decay is zero rather than Hero's 0.02. Losses are not comparable to production. One-rack 4K assignment drops are about twice the eleven-rack observation. Applying the ratios to predict full-run drops is an inference. Long-run routing, evaluation and recovery are untested.

The [later team proposal](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/9615#issuecomment-5923072768) favors a direct **16K** extension rather than the previously planned 8K intermediate step. It proposes 100-step trials of attention scale 1.48/1.58/1.68, with Paloma/UncheatableEval evaluation and gradient monitoring. A follow-up calls for checking learning-rate, weight-decay, mixture, evaluation and token-progress schedules. This changes the public plan under consideration, not confirmed production state. The earlier plan placed the first extension around halfway through training; timing and settings still need explicit launch evidence.

## Belay: the short speed test did not establish stability

The [runtime report](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/9451#issuecomment-5934720837) describes width-512 research runs, not Hero production:

- Flattening train-state array leaves before JIT dispatch reduces narrow `throughput/duration` from **184.3 to 171.6 ms** (6.9%) with similar loss. Actual timestamp-derived elapsed time remains about **191 ms** because that timer omits host work between steps.
- Overlapping consecutive steps gives **171.2 versus 195.8 ms** elapsed time in a 300-step `lof5` test (12.6% less).
- Every full-length overlapping attempt fails around **steps 512–515**, across features/seeds. A 700-step comparison crashes with profiling on and off; depth-zero control completes. The pipeline-depth default returns to zero. This narrows the triggering configuration without identifying the precise failure mechanism.
- Candidate-10 work combines runtime and final-layer changes; a second seed, wider-rung validation and other tests remain in progress. The issue's current-best summary still names candidate 9. No completed full-scale validation or Hero adoption is asserted.

The [parameter-matched layer comparison](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/9451#issuecomment-5925057549) qualifies the earlier output-width explanation: removing a layer's compressed representation while halving expert width produces only about −0.0032 final-layer loss difference, versus −0.0113 when additional parameters were added. Several properties change, so this does not isolate a universal cause; it motivates capacity-controlled follow-ups. Avoid carrying an early causal interpretation forward as fact.

The [edited logit-penalty test](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/issues/9451#issuecomment-5916810072) now contains results. Both four-times-longer width-512 runs remain stable with similar spikes; removal shows no measurable quality gain. The report recommends retaining the penalty and further scale validation before changing that recommendation. The suggestion that matched spikes come from data is an interpretation, not independently proven cause. This is not evidence about removing Hero's stability controls.

Other follow-ups retain their boundaries: #8303/#9495 quality registration and [#9617 tokenizer-prefetch](https://github.com/marin-community/marin/pull/9617) are open, with no production mixture change; #9279/#9460 alternate H100 pipeline/resume, #9353 dependency alignment, #9481 overlap, #9478 rack placement, #9352 query-bias issue and #9374 buffers remain open. Small tokenizer and loss-backward benchmarks do not establish Hero training speedups.
